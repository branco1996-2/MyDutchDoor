Can Americans get a mortgage in the Netherlands?

Yes, US citizens buy homes here. The main differences are the residence-permit rules for non-EU buyers and how each bank handles FATCA.

The short answer

  • Non-EU buyers need a non-temporary residence permit.
  • Lenders typically want roughly six months of living and working here.
  • Each bank sets its own FATCA policy.
  • Banks typically count 90% of non-euro income.

General information, not advice.

Who this guide is for

US citizens who live and work in the Netherlands and want to buy a home here. It also applies to Dutch-American dual nationals ("accidental Americans"): if you hold US citizenship, Dutch banks treat you as a US person.

Residence permit and time here

You need a non-temporary residence permit, one issued for a lasting purpose such as highly skilled migrant work. Lenders typically also want you to have lived and worked here for roughly six months. Exact requirements differ per lender.

FATCA and your US passport

FATCA is a US law that requires banks outside the US to report on accounts held by US persons. Each Dutch lender has chosen its own approach:

Some banksAsk for a US TIN (usually your Social Security number) or a certificate of loss of nationality (CLN)
Other banksLend if you hold a payment account with them
Some non-bank lendersAre not subject to FATCA obligations

Paid in US dollars?

Banks typically count only 90% of non-euro income, converted to euros, when they calculate your maximum mortgage. That buffer covers exchange-rate risk.

The expat ruling: 30% becomes 27%

From 1 January 2027 the tax-free allowance drops from 30% to 27%. People who used the 30% ruling before 2024 fall under transitional rules. How lenders count it — VERIFY

How much can you borrow?

In 2026 you can borrow up to 100% of the purchase value. Your own amount depends on income, debts and the lender's rules; the adviser works that out with you. Purchase costs usually come from savings. VERIFY

What to have ready

  • Passport and residence permit
  • Employment contract and payslips
  • US TIN, or CLN if you have one
  • Recent bank statements

Frequently asked questions

I'm a dual national. Does FATCA apply to me?

Yes. With US citizenship you're a US person for FATCA, even if you've never lived in the US.
