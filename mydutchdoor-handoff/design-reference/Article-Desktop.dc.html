US citizens

Can Americans get a mortgage in the Netherlands?

Yes, US citizens buy homes in the Netherlands. The main differences are the residence-permit rules for non-EU buyers and how each bank handles FATCA. Here's what to expect before you talk to an adviser.

The short answer

  • Non-EU buyers need a non-temporary residence permit.
  • Lenders typically want you to have lived and worked here for roughly six months.
  • Each bank sets its own FATCA policy for US clients.
  • If you're paid in dollars, banks typically count 90% of that income.

This is general information, not advice. An adviser can tell you how it applies to you.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for US citizens who live and work in the Netherlands and want to buy a home here. It also applies to Dutch-American dual nationals, sometimes called "accidental Americans": if you hold US citizenship, Dutch banks treat you as a US person, even if you have never lived in the United States.

Residence permit and time in the Netherlands

As a non-EU buyer you need a non-temporary residence permit. A non-temporary permit is one issued for a purpose the Dutch government treats as lasting, such as highly skilled migrant work, rather than a short stay.

On top of that, lenders typically want you to have lived and worked in the Netherlands for roughly six months before they lend. Exact requirements differ per lender.

FATCA: why your US passport matters to a Dutch bank

FATCA (the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) is a US law that requires banks outside the US to report on accounts held by US persons. Because that reporting takes work, Dutch lenders have each chosen their own approach to American customers:

Some banksA US TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number; for most citizens this is your Social Security number) or a certificate of loss of nationality (CLN)

Paid in US dollars?

Many Americans here are paid in dollars, or partly in dollars. When banks calculate your maximum mortgage, they typically count only 90% of non-euro income, converted to euros. That buffer covers the risk that the exchange rate moves against you.

The expat ruling: 30% becomes 27%

If you're on the expat ruling (often called the 30% ruling), part of your salary is paid tax-free. From 1 January 2027 that tax-free allowance drops from 30% to 27%. People who used the 30% ruling before 2024 fall under transitional rules.

This matters for a mortgage because it can change your net income. How lenders count the allowance — VERIFY with adviser Read more in The expat ruling and your mortgage.

How much can you borrow?

In 2026 you can borrow up to 100% of the purchase value of a home. How much you can borrow in your own case depends on your income, any debts and the lender's rules. Working that out is exactly what the adviser does with you.

Frequently asked questions

I'm a Dutch-American dual national. Does FATCA apply to me?

Yes. If you hold US citizenship you're a US person for FATCA, even if you've never lived in the US. The same lender policies apply to you.
